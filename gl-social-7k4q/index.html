What Level has built, and is adding

  • AEO posts for every tracked question, scheduled from September through January.
  • Articles to back them up, for the website and as LinkedIn articles, with website links added to every post.
  • The brand named in the text. AEO captions now include a line like "Global Lingo supports market research teams in more than 150 languages," because AI answers link brands to services.
  • Key points in the caption, not only in carousels, since carousel text is hard for these tools to read.
  • AEO posts as lead posts. December 4 and 11 move up from backups.

Where the wider business can help

  • Comparison content and listings. AI answers lean on "best providers" lists on other sites, most of which name competitors. Buyer's guides and getting onto those lists sit with content and PR.
  • Named authors for articles (see What we need).
  • Our own data. Poll results, a yearly "in numbers" post, and client results shared with permission give AI tools figures to quote.
  • The same facts everywhere. 150+ languages, the ISO certifications, and the office list should match across the website, LinkedIn, and directories.
  • Mentions on other sites. ESOMAR, the CSA Research Verified 100 listing (which should live on the website too), event pages, and client posts all help.

Ideas worth borrowing, and what to steer away fromdetails

Worth borrowing

  • A named weekly team series. Toppan's #HappyTDLFridays gets their best reactions, up to 75 a post. A Global Lingo equivalent, fed by the photo folder, gives the offices a regular slot.
  • Every open role. Toppan's hiring posts get up to 42 reactions and 7 reposts, and our February careers post was the biggest post of the year.
  • Promotions and milestones. Empower's promotion post got 34 reactions from a page one-ninth our size.
  • Awards, entered on purpose. THG Fluently and Empower were both finalists for Best Research Services Partner at the MRS Operations Awards, and THG won.
  • Business news. Lionbridge's new purchasing agreement was their best recent post (56 reactions).
  • A topic week. Lionbridge ran four posts and an eBook on one subject in a week. A "survey week" around our survey translation guide would help search and AI answers too.

We already post named headshot graphics for events, as Toppan, Lionbridge, and Empower do.

Steer away from

  • Relying on awareness-day graphics. Empower's World Gratitude Day graphic got 6 reactions. These posts still bring in some reach, so when there are no staff photos we'll still post them, just not as the main plan.
  • AI buzzwords and product hype. THG Fluently's "AI-powered localisation" posts got 11 to 13 reactions each.
  • Bare link posts. Lionbridge's article links got 7 to 14 reactions with 387,000 followers.
  • Reposts with nothing added. THG's reshare of a colleague's event post got 3 reactions.
  • Unsourced "studies show" claims, which undercut trust.
  • Posting volume for its own sake. RWS posted 66 times in a month; fewer, stronger posts suit our size.

Growth follows the content, so we can't promise a follower number. What we can promise is to turn every photo, story, and name we're given into the kind of post that performs best.

Time, access, and authors

Time

All 63 website articles are credited to "The Global Lingo Team." Google's guidance asks whether it's clear who wrote a page, and named experts give search engines and AI tools a real person to trust. Authors review the article and put their name to it; they don't write it.

Messages and freelancer enquiries

Leadership
Who looks after the LinkedIn inbox?
Messages to the Global Lingo page need a named owner, so enquiries from clients, partners, and job seekers get answered and passed to the right team.
Vendor management or HR
Where should freelance linguists be sent?
Linguists regularly ask on LinkedIn how to join. The website has a Linguist Registration page. Is that the right route, or is there an email address or portal we should share instead? A standard reply means every enquiry gets the same answer.

Our do's and don'ts for 2027list

Do

  • Post a people post at least every two weeks, with real photos.
  • Share every open role, promotion, work anniversary, and award.
  • Run one playful series each quarter, like the language bracket.
  • Give one article a week a carousel, and send the rest out as link posts with the key points in the caption.
  • Name Global Lingo and the service in every AEO post.
  • Reply to every comment within a day.
  • Post Tuesday to Thursday mornings, and move weekend days to the Friday.
  • Report monthly, and judge AI visibility each quarter.

Don't

  • Post twice on the same day.
  • Rely on awareness-day graphics with no people in them.
  • Use AI buzzwords, fear-based hooks, or unsourced statistics.
  • Reuse the same opening line in posts close together.
  • Post bare links, or reposts with nothing of our own added.
  • Chase posting volume for its own sake.
