what's working.

working

Specific subject lines

For a year, every subject line followed the same "Our [Month] recap" pattern. September's named the three things inside and got the best open rate of the 12 issues.

working

Sent in the working day

All 10 previous issues went out between 4pm and 8:30pm on the last working day of the month. September went at 9:15am on a Wednesday and was opened by more people than any other issue.

working

Articles get the clicks

In August, half the clicks went to the four "worth a read" articles, led by the manufacturing piece. The Global Insights link drew clicks in both issues (2, then 4).

working

A format that repeats

Both issues followed the six-section brief: Karl's note, the month in brief, a named team voice (Andrada, then Liam), reads, a download and a question. Each month now starts from a template.

what we're fixing.

All four fixes work on Global Lingo's current HubSpot plan.

fixing

Photos link to the homepage

In September, 5 of 11 clicks went to the homepage, mostly through the logo and header photo. From October, the header photo links to the lead story (this month, the Global Insights edition), and article images link to their articles. The logo stays on the website.

fixing

No replies to the question

The three answers look like buttons but aren't links. From October, each answer becomes an email link with the answer already filled in, so replying takes two taps. If that proves fiddly, the answer boxes go and the question stays as a plain "hit reply" prompt.

fixing

The download needs a bigger push

The translation brief template got 2 clicks in August and the risk check got none in September. October's download is tied to the lead article and named in the subject line's preview text.

fixing

More skimming in September

63% of September readers glanced rather than read, up from 52.5% in August. September's opening note ran to four paragraphs; October's is one short paragraph.

the contact list.

Bounces, unsubscribes and spam reports depend mostly on who is on the list and how they joined. Global Lingo supplied the list Level now sends to, so these measures are reported for the list's owners. The old list had been shrinking every month: 902 inboxes in August 2025, 510 by April 2026 and 72 in May.

list

Bounces fell after the first send

The first send to the new list bounced at 5.82%. HubSpot automatically stops mailing addresses that hard bounce, and the September send bounced at 0.85%. HubSpot's hard bounce rate for the last 30 days is 0.68%, against a 0.4% benchmark.

list

Unsubscribes are high but falling

22 people left in August and 13 in September (1.81%, then 1.11%), against HubSpot's 0.45% benchmark and 0.28% to 0.89% on the old list.

list

"I didn't sign up"

Across all emails since January 2025, 10 of the 24 people who gave a reason for leaving said they never signed up. That pattern started before the handover.

list

High bounces before August too

HubSpot's May 2026 row shows about 1,200 emails with a 12.18% hard bounce rate, the highest of the 17 months on the dashboard, three months before Level started.

Some opens and clicks come from Global Lingo's own addresses. Four shared global-lingo.com mailboxes (bp@, honda@, global.kineo@ and praxis@) are among the most active openers in both issues, and 2 of the 10 people clicking each month were one of these. Level doesn't know who reads these mailboxes. Without them, about 8 outside readers clicked each issue.

what works for other b2b newsletters.

What email and content researchers say a business newsletter needs, and how The Monthly Brief measures up.

Reward the reader's time with useful things. Readers expect tips, technical information and best practice rather than company promotion. One guide suggests keeping company news to 10% to 20% of the content. The Monthly Brief already leads with practical articles and a download, and keeps company news to the short "in brief" list. Global Lingo's teams can help most here: the tips, questions and lessons that come up in client work are exactly the useful content readers want.

Sources: MediaPost, The Art of the B2B Newsletter; Act-On, 10 ideas for B2B newsletter content

Name the story in the subject line. Subject lines work better when they say what the issue is about rather than just who it's from. September's subject did this and got the best open rate of the year.

Source: MediaPost

Judge success on clicks and replies more than opens. Apple Mail loads email images automatically, which counts as an open even when nobody reads it, so opens are best used to compare issues with each other. Gmail now sorts its Promotions tab by relevance, and engagement such as clicks and replies feeds that. From October, this report leads on clicks and replies.

Sources: Bird, Apple Mail Privacy Protection; MarTech, March 2026

Write in live text, not text inside images. Gmail's Gemini features now summarize emails, and they read the text. Headlines baked into images, and images without alt text (the short description read aloud or shown when images don't load), get lost. The Monthly Brief already does this: every issue uses real text for headers and buttons, with alt text on every image.

Source: Klaviyo, January 2026

Use real people's voices. In the Content Marketing Institute's 2026 research, 96% of B2B organizations create thought leadership, but fewer than 5% of employees typically contribute to it. "From the team" already puts a different named employee in every issue, which most competitors don't do.

Source: Content Marketing Institute, B2B Content and Marketing Trends: Insights for 2026

Invite the reader in. Recurring ideas include answering a real customer question each month with help from sales, polls and quizzes, short customer stories, and a curated "best of the industry" roundup. Some of these suit Global Lingo well; see the next section.

Source: Act-On

Protect deliverability. Gmail and Yahoo require authenticated sending (SPF, DKIM and DMARC), a one-click unsubscribe for marketing mail, and spam complaints below 0.3%. The strict rules apply to senders of about 5,000 emails a day, but following them at any volume helps mail land in the inbox. Spam reports have been 0% on every issue. Global Lingo's IT team can confirm the domain settings.

Source: Bird, Gmail and Yahoo sender requirements

what to add to the newsletter.

Small, repeatable pieces that fit the six-section format and need little extra from the Global Lingo team.

new

Ask Global Lingo

One real question a client asked this month, answered in 60 words by the project manager who handled it. Sales and project teams send one question each month.

new

You told us

Share the replies to last month's question in two lines ("most of you said internal sign-off slows you down"). It shows the question is read and gives readers a reason to answer the next one.

new

A three-line client result

One proof point with a number, used with the client's permission, as the strategy brief already plans. It sits under the download, never as a sales pitch.

new

Where to find us

One line on the next event the team is attending, such as DevLearn in November, so readers can say hello.

new

Word of the month

One untranslatable word with a one-line meaning, matching the popular LinkedIn word posts. It's a light moment readers can share.

new

A downloads library

Once there are four or five downloads, one website page that lists them all, linked from every issue. Each new download then adds to something that lasts.

what we need from global lingo.

Level can keep improving what's in the newsletter. Who receives it, and whether they asked to, needs someone at Global Lingo.

Leadership

Name an owner for the contact list

One person who answers where contacts came from, adds new subscribers, and checks list health before each import.

Data protection lead

Confirm consent for the August list

A quick check that everyone on the supplied list agreed to hear from Global Lingo, or has a relationship that covers it.

Account teams

Decide on the shared mailboxes

Should bp@, honda@, global.kineo@ and praxis@ get the newsletter? If they're internal or client project inboxes, a separate list keeps the numbers clear.

IT team

A sign-up link in email signatures

The website already has a sign-up page. Adding its link to every staff email signature sends more people to it, so the list grows with people who chose to join.

Sales and project teams

One client question a month

For "Ask Global Lingo": a question a client asked, sent by the 15th of each month.

Team leads

Voices and download owners

Three names for "From the team" from November to January, and a team to check each new download.
